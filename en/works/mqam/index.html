MQ-Match* · Subgraph Matching Algorithm

A high-performance C++ subgraph matching algorithm that prunes the search space via symmetry-based isomorphism and multi-graph index co-construction.

Background

Subgraph matching is a foundational problem in graph querying: given a large data graph, find all subgraphs that structurally match a given small query graph. It is NP-complete, with a search space that explodes combinatorially as the node count grows — placing extreme demands on pruning and indexing in any practical implementation.

This project is MQ-Match*, an improved implementation of the existing MQ-Match method, focused on further compressing the search space through symmetry and multi-graph indexing. It creatively draws on strong isomorphism-computation techniques and integrates them into a multi-subgraph matching algorithm.

The paper has now been published in IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering.

Experimental results show that the algorithm is tens of times faster than recent SOTA algorithms, hundreds of times faster than earlier SOTA algorithms, and tens of thousands of times faster than older, non-SOTA multi-subgraph matching algorithms.

The result charts are shown in the right-hand gallery. We evaluate: index construction time, memory footprint, and — most critically — matching time.

What I did

  • Symmetric isomorphism: build symmetry sets via backtracking to identify structural symmetry in the query graph, avoiding redundant searches over equivalent positions.
  • Multi-graph index co-construction: adopt a multi-index architecture (data-graph index + matching tree + symmetry index), rebuilding MQ-Match’s matching tree and adding a symmetry index to jointly cut down the recursion space.
  • Heavily compressed memory: build high-response, low-memory data structures with deep reuse in the DFS, guaranteeing stability and idempotence during recursion and drastically compressing the memory footprint of symmetric structures.

Results

  • We tested the algorithm across multiple datasets, scaling from hundreds of thousands to tens of millions of nodes, and from millions to hundreds of millions of edges. Datasets benchmarked: Youtube, Twitch, Twitter, Flickr, Friendster. Results: tens of times faster than recent SOTA algorithms, hundreds of times faster than earlier SOTA algorithms, and tens of thousands of times faster than older non-SOTA multi-subgraph matching algorithms.
